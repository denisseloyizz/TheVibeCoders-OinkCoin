# Spanish (Mexico) style guide and glossary

Audience: college students in Mexico. Friendly, clear, a little playful. Use **tú**, never vosotros or usted.
Mexican vocabulary: celular, computadora, correo electrónico, contraseña, iniciar sesión, enlace, descargar,
"ahorita" only in jokes. Dollars stay as `$1,234.00` (the app is in USD). Sentence case for labels and buttons.
Keep emoji, numbers, `{placeholders}` and HTML tags (`<b>`, `<strong>`) exactly as they are.
Avoid em dashes and long dashes in the Spanish text; use commas, periods or colons.

## The buddy

Buddy = "tu compañero" in prose, or just use the buddy's name. Piggy = Cochinito. Moods (badge): Happy = Feliz,
Okay = Normal, Sad = Triste, Angry = Enojado, Fainted = Desmayado. Feed = Alimentar, Play = Jugar, Pet = Acariciar.
Hunger/Fun/Love = Hambre/Diversión/Cariño. The buddy talks in first person, cheeky, like a friend.

## Humor

Keep the jokes, but make them land in Mexico: ramen -> sopa instantánea, burrito bowl -> tacos, dining hall -> la cafetería,
Venmo -> transferencia/SPEI, "call mom" -> "háblale a tu mamá". Do not invent slang that might not be understood across Mexico.

## Technical rules

- The key is the exact English text. Do not change English text when wrapping it, or the key stops matching.
- Placeholders such as `{amount}` and `{name}` must appear in the Spanish text, with the same names.
- Do not translate stored data values (categories, goals, moods, character ids). Translate when drawing them.
